## Mobile robot localisation problem

- IMU cannot be used for long-term localisation

- Landmarks are features of the environment

  - Each landmark has a unique location and ID
  - ID can be a high-dimensional vector, in general

- Map: A set of landmarks
  $$
  m = \left\{ (l_x^1, l_y^1, l_z^1), \ (l_x^2, l_y^2, l_z^2), \dots, \ (l_x^s, l_y^s, l_z^s) \right\}
  $$

- Robot's pose at time step $k$ is $X_k = (x_k, y_k, \theta_k)$

- Robot makes a measurement $Z_k = (l_{1k}, l_{2k}, \dots, l_{mk})$

- Localisation problem: Estimate $p(X_k \mid X_0, u_{1:k}, Z_{1:k}, m)$

- SLAM
  - Doing the map building and localisation together
  - Can handle dynamical changes
  
- Handling objects, choosing whether to be a part of the map

- Reducing localisation error by using sensor fusion, from multiple modalities

- We want $p(X_k \mid X_0, u_{1:k}, Z_{1:k}, m)$

  $$
  p(X_k \mid X_0, u_{1:k}, Z_{1:k}, m) = \int p(X_{1:k} \mid X_0, u_{1:k}, Z_{1:k}, m) \, dX_{1:k-1}
  $$

- Probability represented as a collection of samples (particles)

  $$
  \operatorname{bel}(X_k) = \eta_k p(Z_k \mid \mu_{X_k}) \overline{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)}
  $$

  - $\operatorname{bel}(X_k) \sim \{ x_{k-1}^1, x_{k-1}^2, \dots, x_{k-1}^M \}$  be the collection of particles

  - Applying motion model to each particle, we get a new collection of particles

    $$
    \overline{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)} = \int p(X_k \mid X_{k-1}, u_k) \operatorname{bel}(X_{k-1}) \, dX_{k-1}
    $$

  - $\overline{x_k^i} \sim p(X_k \mid X_{k-1} = x_{k-1}^i, u_k)$

- Can numerically estimate any statistic of a random variable, if we have samples from its distribution

  - More samples $\implies$ More accuracy

    $$
    \operatorname{E}[s(X)] = \int s(x) f_X(x) \, dx \sim \frac{1}{N} \sum s(x_i), \quad x_i \sim f_X(x)
    $$

- Any statistic on localisation belief

  $$
  s(X_k) = \int s(X_k) \operatorname{bel}(X_k) \, dX_k = \int s(X_k) \frac{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)}{\overline{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)}} \overline{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)} \, dX_k
  $$

  $$
  \frac{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)}{\overline{\operatorname{bel}(X_k)}} = \eta_k p(Z_k \mid X_k, \mu_k)
  $$

  - Samples of $\operatorname{bel}(X_k)$ can be represented by weighted samples of $\operatorname{bel}(X_{k-1})$

- Weigh an hypothesis (particle) by the measurement likelihood

  $$
  w_k^h = p(Z_k \mid X_k^h)
  $$

  - Hypothesis $p(X_k^h)$
  - Measurements $Z_k$

## Importance sampling

- We want samples of $f$, and we have samples of $g$, so we weight samples of $g$ with $f/g$ to get the same effect as samples of $f$ with equal weights
- Unlikely particles will have very small likelihood weights, and will eventually vanish
- Divergence
  - Never finding the true state, if we don't have particles near the true state
- Collapse
  - Particles near to true state will survive and strengthen but others will die, leading to a collapse $\implies$ Unimodal

## Resampling strategies

- Assume the weights are normalised, i.e., their sum is 1
- Variance of samples can be reduced by a low-variance resampler

### Low-variance resampling

- First sample is uniform between $[0, 1/M)$
- All other samples are equally spaced at $1/M$

### When to resample

- Weights (likelihood) give a good basis for determining health of population
- Compute $N_{\text{eff}}$

$$
N_{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i = 0}^{M - 1}w_k^i}
$$

- If $N_{\text{eff}} < T$, for some threshold, say $M/3$, then we resample

- Otherwise, keep the product of weights per sample per time step

  $$
  w_k^i = p(Z_k \mid X_k^i) w_{k-1}^i
  $$

### Augmentation

- Kidnap robot problem
  - Particles might never come back to true pose
  - Very far off, and might get confused
- Once in a while, augment the particle set with some uniformly random particles
  - Similar to exploration vs exploitation in reinforcement learning
- Rapid changes in the average values of likelihoods would possibly imply that the robot is kidnapped
