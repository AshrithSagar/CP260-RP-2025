## Camera model

- Vision: A very rich modality

### Camera pinhole model

- Camera is modelled as a pinhole
- Light can enter only through the pinhole
- Pinhole: Optical center
- Every pixel has 3 values: RGB, for color cameras
- Frames per second (fps)
  - Every frame is a $W \times H \times 3$ matrix
- Temporal sampling
- Photons emitted from the light source
- Specular reflection
  - Light ray hits a perfect mirror, and angle of incidence equals the angle of relectance
- Parallel processing through GPUs
- Ray tracing
- Just by looking at the image, it is not possible to make out the size of the object
  - Perpective related distortions in monocular images
- Triangle similarity; Ratio of lengths are the same

  $$
  \frac{u}{X} = \frac{v}{Y} = \frac{f}{Z} = \lambda
  $$

- Mathematical model of imaging action

  $$
  p = \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{Z} \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} f_x & s & c_{0x} \\ 0 & f_y & c_{0y} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} }_{\text{Intrinsic parameters}} \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \end{bmatrix}
  $$

  - Mapping a 3D point on a 2D plane
  - Perspective; Projective geometry
  - Homogeneous coordinates
  - A non-linear relationship due to the division

### Camera intrinsic parameters

- 2 DOF in the image pixel
- Ray vector $\begin{bmatrix} \frac{u}{f_x} \\ \frac{v}{f_y} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
- ~ Pixel is like a sensor, telling the direction of which light is coming
  - Every pixel is telling a direction
- Intrinsic matrix $\mathcal{K}$
  - Characteristic of the camera

### Camera extrinsic parameters

- Pose of the camera $\leftrightarrow$ Transform from world frame to the camera frame
- Extrinsic matrix $M_{4 \times 4}$

  $$
  M_{4 \times 4} = \begin{bmatrix} R_{3 \times 3} & t_{3 \times 1} \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \operatorname{SE}(3)
  $$

- Perspective projection matrix $H_{3 \times 4}$

### Projection matrix

$$
H_{3 \times 4} = \begin{bmatrix} K & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

- Has 11 free parameters
- With $p = \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix}$,
  $$
  u = \frac{h_1^\top P}{h_3^\top P}, v = \frac{h_2^\top P}{h_3^\top P}
  $$

- Scaling all elements of $H$ gives the same co-ordinate for $p$
- Given an image measurement, which is a 2D array of points $I = [p]$
- If we know the 3D co-ordinates of the scene points ($P$) and the correspondence with image points, we can
  - Extract $\mathcal{K} \to$ Camera calibration/ Camera intrinsic calibration
  - Extract $\mathcal{M} \to$ Camera pose estimation/ Camera extrinsic calibration
- Projecting from the optical center til the image plane, is the *field of view*
  - Frustrum
- Every pixel in the image plane corresponds to a ~3D pixel in the world
- Object detection and segmentation
- Machine learning
  - Have a highly parametrised function, and train on data
- Not putting semantics out here
- Feature extraction: SIFT, SURF, etc

  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale-invariant_feature_transform>
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speeded_up_robust_features>

- Bundle adjustment in vision/ Visual SLAM in robotics
  - Don't know the values of $\{ P \}$ but know it's associations, then estimating the 3D coordinates and projection matrix

### Camera calibration

- $p_i = H P_i$, where at least 6 points are used
- $P_i$ are fiducial points and their coordinates are known
- $x_i = \frac{h_1^\top P_i}{h_3^\top P_i}, y_i = \frac{h_2^\top P_i}{h_3^\top P_i}$ gives two equations per point
- Unknowns: 12 matrix entries of $H$

  $$
  \implies \begin{bmatrix} - P_i^\top & 0 & x_i P_i^\top \\ & -P_i^\top & y_i P_i^\top \\ & \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} h_1 \\ h_2 \\ h_3 \end{bmatrix} = 0
  \implies D_{12 \times 12} \begin{bmatrix} h_1 \\ h_2 \\ h_3 \end{bmatrix} = 0
  $$

  - Can be solved via SVD of $D$ and finding the singular vector of least singular values.
- Calibration matrix

## 2D-3D: Perspective-n-point (PnP)

- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perspective-n-Point>
- Find the perspective transformation which maps $n$ 3D points to their corresponding image points of the camera

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
u \\ v \\ 1
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
f_x & \gamma & u_0 \\
0 & f_y & v_0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
r_{11} & r_{12} & r_{13} & t_1 \\
r_{21} & r_{22} & r_{23} & t_2 \\
r_{31} & r_{32} & r_{33} & t_3 \\
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
x \\ y \\ z \\ 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

- Pose estimation is an important problem in robotics
- Localisation problem
  - Camera is mounted on the robot, thereby getting the location of the robot
- PnP problem
  - Given $n$ point correspondences, ~can the pose of the robot be known
- P3P
  - Just needs 3 point pairs to solve the problem
  - But upto 4 solutions

$$
\begin{aligned}
& Y^2 + Z^2 - Y Z p - b'^2 = 0 \\
& Z^2 + X^2 - Z X q - c'^2 = 0 \\
& X^2 + Y^2 - X Y r - a'^2 = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

- P4P has a unique solution

- OpenCV's `solvepnp`

### Geometric error formulation

- Minimise the Euclidean distance between the image point $p_i'$ and the projection of the point in the other plane

$$
\arg \min_{H} \sum {d(p_i', H p_i)}^2
$$

- Symmetric transfer error

$$
\arg \min_{H} \sum {d(p_i', H p_i)}^2 + {d(H^{-1} p_i', p_i)}^2
$$

- Reprojection error

$$
\arg \min_{H, \widehat{p_i}, \widehat{p_i'}} \sum {d(p_i', H \widehat{p_i})}^2 + {d(H^{-1} \widehat{p_i'}, p_i)}^2
$$

- Solved by non-linear least squares
- Transform from one image to another using a homography
  - A bijective relationship, can tranform back as well

## RANSAC: Random sample consensus
