## Camera model

- Vision: A very rich modality

### Camera pinhole model

- Camera is modelled as a pinhole
- Light can enter only through the pinhole
- Pinhole: Optical center
- Every pixel has 3 values: RGB, for color cameras
- Frames per second (fps)
  - Every frame is a $W \times H \times 3$ matrix
- Temporal sampling

- Photons emitted from the light source
- Specular reflection
  - Light ray hits a perfect mirror, and angle of incidence equals the angle of relectance
- Parallel processing through GPUs
- Ray tracing
- Just by looking at the image, it is not possible to make out the size of the object
  - Perpective related distortions in monocular images

- Triangle similarity; Ratio of lengths are the same
  $$
  \frac{u}{X} = \frac{v}{Y} = \frac{f}{Z} = \lambda
  $$

- Mathematical model of imaging action
  $$
  p = \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{Z} \underbrace{ \begin{bmatrix} f_x & s & c_{0x} \\ 0 & f_y & c_{0y} \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} }_{\text{Intrinsic parameters}} \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \end{bmatrix}
  $$

  - Mapping a 3D point on a 2D plane
  - Perspective; Projective geometry
  - Homogeneous coordinates
  - A non-linear relationship due to the division

### Camera intrinsic parameters

- 2 DOF in the image pixel
- Ray vector $\begin{bmatrix} \frac{u}{f_x} \\ \frac{v}{f_y} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
- ~ Pixel is like a sensor, telling the direction of which light is coming
  - Every pixel is telling a direction
- Intrinsic matrix $\mathcal{K}$
  - Characteristic of the camera

### Camera extrinsic parameters

- Pose of the camera $\leftrightarrow$ Transform from world frame to the camera frame

- Extrinsic matrix $M_{4 \times 4}$
  $$
  M_{4 \times 4} = \begin{bmatrix} R_{3 \times 3} & t_{3 \times 1} \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
  $$

- Perspective projection matrix $H_{3 \times 4}$

### Projection matrix

$$
H_{3 \times 4} = \begin{bmatrix} K & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} R & t \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
$$

- Has 11 free parameters
- Scaling all elements of $H$ gives the same co-ordinate for $p$
- Given an image measurement, which is a 2D array of points $I = [p]$

- If we know the 3D co-ordinates of the scene points ($P$) and the correspondence with image points, we can
  - Extract $\mathcal{K} \to$ Camera calibration/ Camera intrinsic calibration
  - Extract $\mathcal{M} \to$ Camera pose estimation/ Camera extrinsic calibration
- Projecting from the optical center til the image plane, is the *field of view*
  - Frustrum
- Every pixel in the image plane corresponds to a ~3D pixel in the world
- Object detection and segmentation
- ~Semantics (?)
- Machine learning
  - Have a highly parametrised function, and train on data
