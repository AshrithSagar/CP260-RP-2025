### Harris corner detector

- <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_corner_detector>

- Principle: At a corner, the image intensity will change in multiple directions
- Consider an image patch $I(x, y \in W)$, and a shifted version of it $I(x + u, y + v)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(u, v)
& = \sum_{x, y \in W} {\Big( I(x + u, y + v) - I(x, y) \Big)}^2
\approx \sum_{x, y \in W} {(I_x u + I_y v)}^2 \\
& = \begin{bmatrix} u & v \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \sum_{W} I_x^2 & \sum_{W} I_x I_y \\ \sum_{W} I_x I_y & \sum_{W} I_y^2 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} u & v \end{bmatrix} \ M \ {\begin{bmatrix} u & v \end{bmatrix}}^\top
\end{aligned}
$$

- First-order gradients

- HCD is rotation, translation brightness invariant but not scale invariant.

- Eigenvalues of $M$ determine
  - If the error is large in all directions $\implies$ a corner
  - In case one eigenvalue is much larger than the other $\implies$ an edge
  - In both eigenvalues are small $\implies$ not a corner or edge feature

- Using SVD

- $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are the eigenvalues of $M$
  $$
  R = \det M - k \operatorname{trace}(M)^2
  $$

  - $R$ is used to score a corner

- Non-maximum suppression

  - ~Multiple points also detected along an edge.
  - Find local maxima in a $3\times 3$ window around the center pixel

#### Shi Tomasi

$$
R = \min (\lambda_1, \lambda_2)
$$

- All these are implemented in OpenCV

### Scale invariance

- Scale changes, for example, when the camera moves

- Blur the image first by a Gaussian filter

  $$
  L(x, y, \sigma) = G(x, y, \sigma) * I(x, y)
  $$

  - $G \to$ Gaussian kernel

- Found empirically that the extrema for $\sigma^2 \nabla^2 G * I(x, y)$ provides the most stable features

- For Gaussian kernels,

  $$
  \sigma \nabla^2 G = \frac{\partial G}{\partial x} \approx L(x, y, k \sigma) - L(x, y, \sigma) = D(x, y, \sigma)
  $$

  - $D(x, y, \sigma) \to$ Difference of Gaussians

### FAST feature detector

- Consider 16 pixels around a point.
- If at least $n$ pixels around $p$ in the ring are either brighter or darker than $p$ by a threshold, then it is a corner feature.
- Choosing the order of comparisons among the 16 pixels
  - A division problem
  - Use a decision tree. Train a DT using representative images. The DT is compiled into C code for fast execution.
